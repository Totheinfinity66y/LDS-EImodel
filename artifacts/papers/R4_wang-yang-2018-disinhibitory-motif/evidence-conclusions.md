## Directly reported evidence and conclusions

1. **The problem framed as routing.** The review defines “pathway gating” as the need to route information to the right place at the right time in a brain where each cortical area receives from and projects to dozens of areas, and lists as its motivation the new high-quality inter-areal connectivity datasets for macaque and mouse (PDF pp. 1–2). It states the thesis: a disinhibitory motif realized by three non-overlapping interneuron subclasses (PV+ targeting perisomatic regions and controlling spiking output; SST+ targeting dendrites and controlling inputs; VIP+ preferentially targeting SST cells) is a plausible circuit substrate for that routing (PDF p. 2).
2. **The motif’s origin and its predictions.** The review traces the motif to the 2004 Wang, Tegnér, Constantinidis & Goldman-Rakic working-memory circuit model, which was motivated by three anatomical observations (VIP/CR cells preferentially target other interneurons; VIP/CR preferentially target CB/SST rather than PV; CB/CR predominate in prefrontal cortex) and produced predictions later supported by single-unit recordings in behaving monkeys — notably that dendrite-targeting interneurons show spontaneous activity and “inverted tuning” — and by mouse work in which VIP versus SST activation respectively enhanced and impaired working-memory retention (PDF pp. 2–3). The review quotes Wang et al.’s own emphasis that the three types should be read by synaptic target rather than by calcium-binding protein expression (PDF p. 3).
3. **Layer and state dependence.** The review reports that in layer 2/3 of mouse S1, locomotion onset correlates with increased pyramidal and VIP activity and decreased SST activity, whereas in deep layers SST activity increases during whisking instead, which it attributes to laminar differences in VIP→SST projection strength; it also notes that slice multi-cell recordings show layers of a local circuit can occupy markedly different dynamical regimes (PDF p. 3).
4. **Four requirements for pathway gating.** The review states the motif must satisfy four conditions: nonlinear dendritic responses so SST-mediated inhibition matters; segregation of inputs from different pathways onto different parts of the dendritic tree; SST cells able to exert selective control over individual branches; and alignment between pathway clustering and branch-specific disinhibition (PDF p. 4). It explicitly flags that direct experimental evidence for pathway-specific synaptic clustering is still lacking, while citing Wilson et al. for feature-specific clustering in V1 and Cichon & Gan for branch-specific activity and its loss under SST inactivation (PDF p. 4).
5. **The Yang et al. model result.** The review reports that the 2016 computational work (C1 in this repository) satisfied all four requirements with minimal assumptions, and — most consequentially — that tailored SST→pyramidal and VIP→SST connections are unnecessary: random connectivity plus random SST activation/deactivation suffices for branch-specific inhibition/disinhibition, because SST→dendrite connectivity is sparse relative to dense neuron-to-neuron connectivity. Alignment of excitation with disinhibition can then emerge from synaptic plasticity (PDF pp. 4–5).
6. **Advantages over an excitatory implementation.** The review argues that disinhibitory gating is computationally preferable: an excitatory gate depolarizes the dendrite and compresses its dynamic range, whereas a disinhibitory gate leaves dendrites inhibited by default and widens the range; and disinhibition is permissive, so control inputs cannot by themselves activate dendrites, avoiding spurious responses from over-strong control (PDF p. 5).
7. **Anatomical gradient as support.** Citing Kim et al., the review reports a more than 7-fold change in the SST/PV balance across mouse cortex, with SST less abundant than PV in primary somatosensory and motor areas but 3–5 times more abundant than PV in higher-order areas such as prelimbic cortex; it reads this as consistent with association areas needing more input gating (PDF p. 5, Fig. 3 caption, PDF p. 13).
8. **Alternative and complementary gating mechanisms.** The review contrasts input gating with (i) excitatory–inhibitory balance that must be broken to “gate in”, (ii) synchrony-dependent gating, (iii) output gating via chandelier cells that target the axon initial segment of projection-specific pyramidal neurons in mouse prelimbic cortex, and (iv) recurrent gating, citing the Mante et al. context-dependent recurrent dynamics study (C2 in this repository) and noting Yang et al. showed the same task can be achieved with moderate input gating. It states that circuit-level experimental support for subcortical gating via thalamus and basal ganglia is still lacking, and distinguishes pathway gating from gating within one pathway such as spatial attention (PDF pp. 5–6, Fig. 4 caption, PDF p. 14).
9. **The decisive test the review asks for.** The model predicts that cognitive control signals such as behavioral rule or context should act by targeting VIP- and SST-expressing interneurons rather than pyramidal cells; the review states that a direct test requires cell-type-specific recording and manipulation in animals performing tasks that depend on flexible routing, and that such progress would bear on psychiatric deficits (PDF p. 7).

## Evidence boundary

The review argues for plausibility and consistency of a circuit substrate, not for a demonstrated mechanism. Its core requirement — segregation of different pathways onto different dendritic branches — is explicitly stated to lack direct experimental support, and its strongest positive evidence is a computational model (C1) whose assumptions the review itself characterizes as minimal. The output-gating proposal is explicitly labelled as awaiting cell-type-specific connectivity evidence, and subcortical gating is explicitly labelled as lacking circuit-level support. Any use of this review to justify a specific implementation in a model should carry the caveat that the distinguishing requirement has not been measured.
